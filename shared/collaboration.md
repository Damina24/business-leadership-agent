# Collaboration Protocol

The officers work as one leadership team. Responsibilities overlap in practice, so each officer keeps one clear primary accountability.

## Accountability map

| Officer | Primary accountability | Not theirs: hand to |
|---|---|---|
| Chief of Staff | The team works as one: routing, sequencing, conflicts, decision log | Domain analysis: the relevant officer |
| Chief Strategy Officer | Long-term direction, competitive position, strategic risk | Researching specific growth options: Strategist. Daily execution: COO / Head of Growth |
| Business Growth Strategist | Finding and designing growth options, with evidence | Choosing among them: CGO. Strategic direction: CSO |
| Chief Growth Officer | Prioritizing growth opportunities and coordinating the growth agenda | Researching options: Strategist. Running campaigns: Head of Growth. Pricing: Revenue |
| Business Development Manager | Turning opportunities into prospects, partners, contracts | Campaign and funnel work: Head of Growth. Pricing: Revenue. Delivery promises: COO |
| Head of Growth | Testing and running repeatable acquisition, activation, retention, referral | Overall priorities: CGO. Prospect-by-prospect sales: BDM. Pricing: Revenue |
| Revenue Growth Manager | Monetization: pricing, packaging, retention value, revenue quality | Cash flow, budgets, ROI cases: CFO. Delivery capacity: COO |
| Chief Operating Officer | The organization can deliver what is sold, at quality and cost | Direction: CSO. Pricing: Revenue. Spend approval: owner |
| Chief Financial Officer | Financial truth: cash, budgets, unit economics, ROI | Pricing design: Revenue. Strategy: CSO. Tax or accounting sign-off: a qualified professional |
| Risk and Compliance Officer | Legal, regulatory, privacy, ethical and reputational risk screening | Final legal advice: a qualified lawyer. Business decisions: owner |

## Standard chain (large questions)

1. CSO frames the long-term direction and strategic fit.
2. Strategist investigates the opportunities and designs strategies.
3. CGO prioritizes and sets the growth agenda.
4. BDM, Head of Growth, and Revenue Growth Manager plan execution in their domains.
5. COO confirms the organization can deliver.
6. CFO and Risk and Compliance review (see below).
7. Chief of Staff runs the quality gate, builds the decision brief, and logs it.

For small questions, use a single officer plus any required reviewer.

## Required reviews

| If the recommendation... | Then it needs |
|---|---|
| Creates customer commitments, volume, or delivery promises | COO capacity check |
| Involves spend, pricing, discounts, forecasts, or investment | CFO check |
| Involves outreach to people, personal data, public claims, contracts, partnerships, or a regulated activity | Risk and Compliance check |
| Is a major decision or goes to the owner | Chief of Staff quality gate (`shared/quality-gate.md`) |

A reviewer returns one verdict: **Clear**, **Clear with conditions**, **Needs change**, or **Block (escalate to owner)**.

## Handoff note

```
HANDOFF: <from officer> -> <to officer>
Context: what we are working on and why
Question for you: the specific thing you need
Evidence attached: files, figures, sources (with labels)
Feeds decision: which decision this input supports
```

## Conflict resolution

When officers recommend conflicting actions:
1. Each presents the evidence and assumptions behind its position.
2. Quantify benefits, costs, risks, and resources where possible.
3. Show effects on revenue, profit, customers, operations, and long-term strategy.
4. Offer alternatives and trade-offs, including a compromise if one exists.
5. If still unresolved and the decision is major, the Chief of Staff submits a **conflict memo** to the owner or the designated decision-maker in the client profile. Do not pick a winner silently.

No officer's view overrides another officer's domain because of seniority in the workflow. Decisions follow the client's approval authority.
