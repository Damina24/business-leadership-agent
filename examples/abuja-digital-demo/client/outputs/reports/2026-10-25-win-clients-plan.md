# Win-Clients Plan: First 90 Days

## 1. Bottom line
Start with one entry offer, the Starter Website (₦350,000), and use it to build Care Plan subscriptions, which are the most profitable use of the team's hours. Hold off on Business Web Apps until capacity and cash allow. In the base case the company signs 6 website clients in 90 days, never uses more than 84% of plannable hours, and reaches monthly cash break-even in month 9 with cash never below about ₦2.7 million. The main risk is runway. If sales run at about half the base case, cash runs out in month 8, so the plan includes a month-3 checkpoint. **Data confidence is Low:** every company figure is an unverified owner estimate.

## 3. Analysis by officer

### Business Development Manager
- The ideal customer profile is a hypothesis (schools, clinics, professional firms, WhatsApp-led retailers; budget ₦300,000 to ₦2,000,000). It will be rewritten after the first three clients.
- The pipeline has 2 real warm contacts and **0 invented entries**. A sourcing plan targets 30 prospects to research by 2026-11-15, each with a real source and date. See `prospect-pipeline.md`.
- Outreach is drafted only. The founder approves and sends.

### Head of Growth
- Three tests fit the ₦150,000 monthly budget: a free digital audit (E-01), referral partners (E-02, starting in month 2), and a Google Business Profile with real case studies (E-03). See `experiment-backlog.md`.
- Every test has a success and a stop threshold. With few results, outcomes are directional, not proof.
- Measure cost per qualified conversation and per signed project, not enquiries or followers.

### Revenue Growth Manager
- **Offer ladder:** Starter Website to Care Plan (pitched at delivery), then Business Web App later. Care Plans earn about twice the margin per hour of a website (₦10,500 against ₦5,083).
- **Pricing is not validated.** Use cost as the floor and test prices with the first five quotes, recording accepted or rejected and the reason. Review prices quarterly, because inflation is 14.63%.
- **Discount maths** (website margin 87.1%): a 10% discount (₦35,000) needs about 13% more volume to keep the same profit, and a 20% discount (₦70,000) needs about 30%. Capacity is limited and leads are few, so extra volume is unlikely. Recommendation: 10% only for the first 3 clients, in exchange for permission to publish a real case study. The founder must approve. See the conflict memo.
- **Payment terms:** 50% to start, 50% on delivery.

### Chief Operating Officer (capacity check)
- Base-case hours per month: 182 in month 3, 198 in month 6, 216 in month 9, against the 256-hour plannable ceiling. Peak use is 84% of the ceiling.
- A Business Web App takes about 200 hours a month for two months. Added to even one new website (60 hours) it breaks the ceiling (260 hours).
- The founder is both lead developer and only salesperson. The audits use selling time (20 of 80 non-delivery hours).
- All delivery hours are guesses until the first two jobs are timed.

### Chief Financial Officer
Simplified monthly cash model (ESTIMATE): 50% of each project paid at signing and 50% a month later, Care Plans start two months after signing, the first 3 websites carry the 10% discount, and fixed costs and the growth budget are paid every month. No tax, late payments, or owner pay increases are included.

| Case | Websites signed per month | Care Plan take-up | Lowest cash | First month cash in at least cash out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 1, 2, then 3 a month | 50% of clients | ₦2,741,000 in month 8 | Month 9 (₦1,500,000 in, ₦1,457,000 out) |
| Downside | 0, 1, 1, then 2 a month | 30% of clients | ₦8,800 at the end of month 7, then negative (-₦530,400 in month 8) | Not reached in 9 months |

The company cannot reach break-even on websites alone at three a month (₦915,000 contribution against ₦1,250,000 needed), so it relies on Care Plans building up. **Runway, not demand, is the binding constraint.**

### Risk and Compliance Officer
- **Outreach:** Clear with conditions. Business contacts only, with source recorded, sender identified, a working opt-out, and a do-not-contact list.
- **Claims and testimonials:** no "best" or results claims without proof; testimonials only from real clients, with written permission.
- **Referral fees (E-02):** written agreement, and referrers must disclose the fee to the prospect.
- **Data protection:** the company will hold client and customer personal data. The NDPC guidance notice says controllers and processors "of major importance" must register, and it names factors including handling more than 200 data subjects and cloud computing. The fee tier and deadline for a firm like this are unclear in the source reviewed. **Needs professional review** and a check on ndpc.gov.ng.
- **Contracts:** have a Nigerian lawyer review the contract template before the first signature (IP ownership, payment, liability, maintenance scope, data processing).

## 5. Risks and dependencies
- Runway is short (4.8 months with no revenue). The plan depends on early cash from the first projects.
- All delivery hours and prices are unverified.
- The founder is a single point of failure (developer and salesperson).
- A Business Web App would take most of the team's capacity for two months.
- Inflation can raise hosting and tool costs. Review costs quarterly.
