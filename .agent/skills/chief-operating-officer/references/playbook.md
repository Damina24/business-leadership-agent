# COO Playbook

## Capacity check (run on any new commitment)
Verdicts: **Go**, **Go with conditions**, **No-go for now**.

1. What is being promised: volume, scope, deadline, quality level.
2. Current capacity (VERIFIED from profile or data): people x productive hours, divided by hours per unit of work.
3. Current load, plus the new load. Utilization = demand / capacity.
4. Utilization above roughly 80-85% leaves little room for errors and spikes, so flag it as a risk (heuristic).
5. What must change to deliver: hiring, tools, subcontractors, process. Cost and lead time (hand to CFO).
6. Quality and delivery risks if volume rises.
7. Verdict with conditions (for example "accept up to N customers per month until a second delivery person is in place").

## Formulas
- Capacity per period = people x productive hours per person / hours per unit
- Utilization = workload / capacity
- On-time delivery rate = on-time deliveries / total deliveries
- Error or rework rate = jobs needing rework / total jobs
- Productivity = output / labor hours
- Cost per unit = total delivery cost / units delivered

## Bottleneck method
Map the process end to end. Time each step. The slowest step with a queue in front of it is the bottleneck. Improve that step first, because improving other steps does not raise output.

## Responsibility matrix (RACI)
For each process step: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. One accountable person per step.

## Operational risk register
Risk, cause, likelihood, impact, early signal, mitigation or contingency, owner, review date.

## Automation candidates (score each)
Frequency of task x time saved per run x error reduction, compared with the effort and the cost of the tool. Automate stable, repetitive, rule-based work first, and document the process before automating it.

## Operations dashboard (start small)
On-time delivery, error rate, utilization, backlog, customer satisfaction, cost per unit. Use whatever the client can actually measure, and mark the gaps.
