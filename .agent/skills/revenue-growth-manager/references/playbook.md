# Revenue Growth Manager Playbook

## Unit economics formulas
- Gross margin % = (revenue - cost of delivery) / revenue
- Contribution margin = revenue - variable costs (including delivery, payment fees, commissions)
- Average revenue per customer (ARPC) = revenue / customers in the period
- Churn rate = customers lost in period / customers at start of period
- Lifetime value (LTV) = ARPC per period x gross margin % / churn rate per period
- CAC payback (periods) = CAC / (ARPC per period x gross margin %)
- LTV : CAC ratio. Many businesses aim for 3 or higher, so treat that as a rule of thumb and check it against the client's cash position.

## Discount maths (use before approving any discount)
A price cut of d (as a share of price) on a product with gross margin m (as a share of price) needs
required volume increase = d / (m - d)
Example: 10% cut at 40% margin needs 0.10 / 0.30 = 33% more volume to keep the same gross profit. If m <= d, the discount loses money on every sale.

## Revenue stream analysis table
Stream, revenue, share of total, gross margin, growth, customers, repeat rate, trend, verdict (grow, fix, harvest, stop).

## Pricing approaches (pick based on evidence)
- **Cost-plus:** floor only. Never ignore customer value.
- **Value-based:** price against the outcome the customer gets. Needs customer evidence.
- **Competitor-referenced:** context, not a strategy.
- **Tiering / packaging:** good-better-best, anchored on the offer most customers should pick.
- **Recurring models:** retainer, subscription, usage-based. Check cash timing and delivery capacity.

## Pricing test protocol
1. Hypothesis and metric (conversion, margin, retention).
2. Test on a limited segment or period, and keep the old price as control.
3. Pre-agree success and stop thresholds, and get owner approval.
4. Watch margin and churn, not only conversion.
5. Record the result in the decision log.

## Revenue leakage checklist
- [ ] Work delivered but not invoiced, or scope creep given free
- [ ] Unapproved or stacking discounts
- [ ] Late or unpaid invoices, and weak collection follow-up
- [ ] Failed or lapsed recurring payments
- [ ] Customers on old prices after costs rose
- [ ] Low-margin products or customers consuming capacity
- [ ] Missed renewals, upsells, and referrals
- [ ] Refunds, returns, and write-offs that are not tracked

## Forecast method
Build bottom-up from drivers (customers x price x frequency x retention). Give low, base, and high cases and list the assumptions with labels. Compare past forecasts with actuals to improve accuracy.

## Output: pricing recommendation
Current price and margin, recommended change, evidence, expected effect on volume and profit (with discount maths), risks, test plan, approval needed from.
