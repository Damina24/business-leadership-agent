# CGO Playbook

## Opportunity scoring model
Score each opportunity 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) per criterion, multiply by the weight, and sum. Max score is 50. Treat the score as a discussion aid, not the answer. Explain the top three in words.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Profit impact | 3 | How much profit (not revenue) could this add? |
| Evidence strength | 2 | How much VERIFIED data supports the demand and return? |
| Speed to impact | 1 | How soon will it show results? |
| Ease / low cost | 2 | How cheap and simple is it to start? |
| Strategic fit | 1 | Does it support the long-term direction (CSO)? |
| Low risk | 1 | Legal, reputational, operational, financial risk |

## Growth plan template
- **North-star metric** and the 2-4 input metrics that drive it.
- **Objectives (3-5):** objective, KPI, baseline, target, deadline, owner.
- **Initiatives** per objective, ranked, with cost, expected impact (with label), timeline.
- **Investment allocation:** a common starting heuristic is about 70% core business, 20% adjacent growth, 10% experiments. This is a heuristic, so adjust it to the client's risk appetite and cash position.
- **Dependencies:** capacity (COO), budget (CFO), compliance.
- **Review cadence:** monthly KPI review, quarterly reset.

## Executive growth review template
1. Scorecard: KPI, target, actual, trend, status (on track / at risk / off track)
2. What worked, and why (evidence)
3. What did not, and why
4. Barriers to growth
5. Corrective actions (owner, date)
6. Decisions needed from the owner

## KPI formulas
- Revenue growth rate = (current period revenue - prior period revenue) / prior period revenue
- Profitability improvement = change in net margin, in percentage points
- Customer retention = customers at end who were also customers at start / customers at start
- Return on growth investment = (incremental gross profit - cost of initiative) / cost of initiative
- Market expansion = new segments or regions with at least one paying customer

## Common pitfalls
- Chasing many opportunities at once. Limit active initiatives to what capacity allows.
- Targets with no baseline. Get the baseline first.
- Counting revenue from a discount-driven spike as growth.
