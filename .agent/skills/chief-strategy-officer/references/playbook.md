# CSO Playbook

## Frameworks (use the lightest one that answers the question)
- **SWOT:** keep each item specific and evidenced. Convert into actions (use strengths on opportunities, fix weaknesses that expose threats).
- **PESTLE:** political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental forces. Only list those that materially affect this client.
- **Five forces:** rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. Say what each means for margins.
- **Three horizons:** H1 defend and extend the core, H2 build emerging businesses, H3 explore options.
- **Scenarios:** 2-3 plausible futures with early-warning signals and the client's response to each.

## Strategic fit test (for any major decision)
1. Does it serve the mission and long-term objectives?
2. Does it build or use a real, hard-to-copy advantage?
3. Can the organization afford the downside? (CFO)
4. Can it be delivered without hurting the core? (COO)
5. Is it reversible? If not, require stronger evidence.
6. What would make us stop? (kill criteria)

## Strategic risk register
Risk, cause, likelihood (L/M/H), impact (L/M/H), early-warning signal, mitigation, owner, review date.

## Strategic decision memo
```
Decision: <what is being decided, by when, by whom>
Context: <what changed or what was proposed>
Options: <A, B, "do nothing">: benefits, costs, risks, evidence labels
Fit test result:
Recommendation and rationale:
Conditions and kill criteria:
Reviews: CFO / COO / Compliance verdicts
Next review date:
```

## Roadmap structure
Vision (3-5 years) -> strategic priorities (max 3-5) -> 12-month objectives -> initiatives (owner, resource, milestone) -> signals that would change the plan.

## Common pitfalls
- A strategy that is a list of wishes with no trade-offs. A real strategy says what NOT to do.
- Copying competitors without a reason the client can win.
- Long-term plans with no review trigger.
